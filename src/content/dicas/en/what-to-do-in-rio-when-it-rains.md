People who come to Rio tend to pack for the sun. But it does rain here, sometimes heavily, above all between December and March. When the day starts out grey, I do not cancel anything. I change the itinerary.

The first rule is simple: on a rainy day, viewpoints are not worth it. Christ the Redeemer and Sugarloaf can sit inside the cloud, and you pay the ticket to look at white. Whenever I can, I move those two to another day.

## The historic center saves the day

Downtown Rio was made for days like this. The Royal Portuguese Reading Room is one of the most beautiful libraries in the world, and the grey light coming through the stained glass suits it. A few minutes away is São Bento Monastery, with an interior covered in gilded woodwork. And the Metropolitan Cathedral, which looks like raw concrete from outside and holds four enormous stained glass panels that only really work once you are standing inside.

All of it is close together, mostly under cover, and completely independent of the weather.

## Museums

Rio has museums that are worth the trip on their own. The Museum of Tomorrow, on the waterfront, and the Rio Art Museum, on the same square, work well together in one morning. The National Historical Museum and the National Museum of Fine Arts are downtown and quieter.

## Maracanã

The stadium tour is almost entirely under cover. You go into the dressing room, walk along the edge of the pitch and pass through the trophy room. If you like football, a rainy day is a good excuse.

## Eating without hurry

Rain is the best time for the Rio of long meals. The Municipal Market, the old downtown pastry shops and the bars of Santa Teresa all gain something when it is wet outside. I tend to stretch lunch on these days, because there is no point rushing.

## One thing I have learned

Rain in Rio almost never lasts the whole day. It often closes in during the morning and clears at three in the afternoon. So I build a rainy day with an open window at the end: if the sky clears, there is still time to go somewhere high and see the city freshly washed, which is when it looks its best.
