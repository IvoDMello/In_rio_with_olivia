Rio faces south and is full of hills, which means that at the end of the day there is always one spot better than the others, and which one it is changes with the month. These are the five I use most.

## Arpoador

The rock at the far end of Ipanema is the best known sunset in the city, and deservedly so. The sun drops behind the Dois Irmãos peaks and people applaud when it touches the water. Arrive about forty minutes early, because it fills up. It works best between April and September, when the sun sets more in line with the hills.

## Sugarloaf

Going up late in the day costs the same as going up at noon and gives you two landscapes: the city in daylight and the city lit up. Spend a little time at the Morro da Urca station looking towards Botafogo, then carry on to the top. This is the one I recommend most to anyone with a single free afternoon.

## Dona Marta Viewpoint

From here you see Christ the Redeemer on one side and Guanabara Bay on the other, with the whole city in between. It is one of the few places where Rio fits into a single photograph. Since it is high up and quick to visit, it slots in easily after something else.

## Mirante do Leblon

The left hand end of Leblon has a small viewpoint, almost always less crowded than Arpoador, with the same view of the Dois Irmãos. I take people there when they want the sunset without the crowd.

## Rodrigo de Freitas Lagoon

The sunset at the lagoon is a different kind of plan. The light hits Corcovado and the surface of the water at the same time. It is flat, you can walk or sit at a kiosk, and it works well with children and with anyone who is already tired.

## How to choose

If it is your only afternoon, Sugarloaf. If you want the Rio the locals know, Arpoador. If you want the photograph that explains the city, Dona Marta. If you want quiet, Leblon. If you want to sit down with a drink, the lagoon.

And always check the time of sunset on the day of your visit. In Rio the sun goes down around 5.30pm in June and around 6.45pm in December, which changes the shape of the whole day.
