Three days is the length of stay I hear most often from people coming to Rio for the first time. It is enough to get to know the city properly, as long as the itinerary respects two things: the distances and your own pace. This is the route I usually suggest, and then adjust for each traveler.

## Day one: the high city

I start with Christ the Redeemer. Not only for the view. It is because from up there you can read the geography of the whole city, and the rest of the trip makes much more sense afterwards. I pick the time of the visit together with you, looking at the forecast and at what comes before and after it in the day.

On the way down I stop in Santa Teresa. It is one of the oldest neighborhoods in Rio, with cobbled streets, old mansions and artists' studios. We have lunch there. In the afternoon we walk down the Selarón Steps and carry on to the historic center, where you find the Metropolitan Cathedral, the Royal Portuguese Reading Room and São Bento Monastery. Three buildings that could not be more different from one another, and all within a few minutes' walk.

## Day two: the sea

Sugarloaf comes in here, morning or afternoon, whichever fits your day better. Two cable cars, two landscapes, and a view that pays off at any hour. What really makes the difference is a clear sky, so I tend to save this one for the most open day of your trip.

Then the beach. Copacabana, Ipanema and Leblon are three beaches in a row, and each one has its own crowd and its own rhythm. Walking the promenade between them is about as carioca as it gets. On the way back we pass the Rodrigo de Freitas Lagoon and, if there is still time, the Botanical Garden.

And Arpoador, the rock between Ipanema and Copacabana, rounds the day off nicely. From there you see Ipanema on one side, Copacabana on the other and the Dois Irmãos peaks on the horizon. It is a short stop that fits in at any point of the route.

## Day three: whichever Rio you choose

The third day is the most personal one, and this is where itineraries part ways.

If you want nature, we go to the Tijuca Forest, one of the largest urban forests in the world, with a waterfall and viewpoints inside the city. If you would rather understand Rio from another angle, we can visit a community, with local hosts. If football is what moves you, the Maracanã. And if you simply want to breathe, Parque Lage, at the foot of Corcovado, is one of the loveliest places I know.

## What I would change

If you come between December and March, I adjust the order of the stops because of the midday heat. If you come in winter, I make the most of the better visibility and save Christ the Redeemer and Sugarloaf for the most open days. You do not have to worry about any of this. Fitting it together is my job.

And if three days sounds short, it is. But you can still leave with the feeling of having known Rio, rather than just passed through it.
