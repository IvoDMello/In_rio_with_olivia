Three days is the length of stay I hear most often from people coming to Rio for the first time. It is enough to get to know the city properly, as long as the itinerary respects two things: the distances and the hours of good light. This is the route I usually suggest, and then adjust for each traveler.

## Day one: the high city

I start with Christ the Redeemer, early in the morning. Not only for the view. It is because by then the mist has lifted off Corcovado and the platform is not yet crowded. From up there you can read the geography of the whole city, and the rest of the trip makes much more sense afterwards.

On the way down I stop in Santa Teresa. It is one of the oldest neighborhoods in Rio, with cobbled streets, old mansions and artists' studios. We have lunch there. In the afternoon we walk down the Selarón Steps and carry on to the historic center, where you find the Metropolitan Cathedral, the Royal Portuguese Reading Room and São Bento Monastery. Three buildings that could not be more different from one another, and all within a few minutes' walk.

## Day two: the sea

Sugarloaf can be the morning plan, but I like to flip it when I can: go up in the late afternoon and watch the city light up. If you only get one chance, pick the clearest day, because up there the view is everything.

Then the beach. Copacabana, Ipanema and Leblon are three beaches in a row, and each one has its own crowd and its own rhythm. Walking the promenade between them is about as carioca as it gets. On the way back we pass the Rodrigo de Freitas Lagoon and, if there is still time, the Botanical Garden.

The end of the day belongs to Arpoador, the rock between Ipanema and Copacabana. The sun sets exactly behind the Dois Irmãos peaks and people applaud. That is not an exaggeration. It happens every day.

## Day three: whichever Rio you choose

The third day is the most personal one, and this is where itineraries part ways.

If you want nature, we go to the Tijuca Forest, one of the largest urban forests in the world, with a waterfall and viewpoints inside the city. If you would rather understand Rio from another angle, we can visit a community, with local hosts. If football is what moves you, the Maracanã. And if you simply want to breathe, Parque Lage, at the foot of Corcovado, is one of the loveliest places I know.

## What I would change

If you come between December and March, swap the mornings and the afternoons. The midday heat is strong and the beach works better early. If you come in winter, make the most of the better visibility and save Christ the Redeemer and Sugarloaf for the clearest days.

And if three days sounds short, it is. But you can still leave with the feeling of having known Rio, rather than just passed through it.
